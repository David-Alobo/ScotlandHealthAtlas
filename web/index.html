Same country, different risks

Where you live shapes how often you end up in A&E and your risk of dying of poisoning. How old you are shapes your risk from a fall. Two different problems, two different groups of people, one health service.

Two kinds of injury death

Unintentional injuries kill about as many people through poisoning ( a year) as through falls (). They are not the same people.

Falls are deaths of old age: of those who die after a fall are 75 or older, and are women.

Poisoning kills people in mid-life: are aged 25 to 64, and are men. The data does not say which substance; drug-related deaths are the likely main part, but this is a reading, not a finding.

What this can and cannot say

  • Deprivation here describes neighbourhoods, not each person's circumstances.
  • A&E figures count visits, not people.
  • Council comparisons compare areas; they do not show that deprived people are the ones who die.
  • Prescriptions are counted where they are dispensed, which is not always where people live.
  • The injury figures reproduce Public Health Scotland's published totals (deaths exactly, admissions within 2%).
